The Photograph Was the Product: The Credit Trail in Banksy's Own Books

A documented unit, built from the credit pages of four Banksy books. Where a claim rests on the books themselves it is marked DOCUMENTED. Where it rests on biography or interpretation it is marked UNVERIFIED or CODEX, and is kept separate.

Companion to "Another Banksy Market Test" (./another-banksy-market-test.md), first published on Reddit, r/Banksy, 30 June 2026: https://www.reddit.com/r/Banksy/comments/1uj4t6j/another_banksy_market_test_the_signature_no_one/ . That essay's appendix points here for the documented foundation under its authorship question.

The argument in one line

For street art, the saleable object at origin is not the wall, which is left to degrade or be removed, but the photograph of the wall. So the question of who holds the photography credit in the early books is the question of who holds the original asset. The books answer it, and the answer changes over time, from the photographer alone toward "Banksy" included.

DOCUMENTED (all four pages now in hand): across Banging Your Head, Existencilism, Cut It Out, and Wall and Piece the photographic image is credited, when credited at all, to a sole named photographer (Lazarides), then a four-name "additional photography" line beside "artwork by Banksy," then no separate credit, then a six-name "additional photography" line. What it never becomes, across four books, is a single authored eye. The photograph of the work is a many-handed product every time the book credits it, and the artwork credit sits separate from it throughout. That is the load-bearing fact, and it is now closed on all four colophons rather than three plus a guess.

Why the progression matters

If the product at the street level is the photograph, then the earliest book credits the saleable image to a photographer and not to "Banksy." The author of record for the image, around 2001, is Lazarides alone. Existencilism then splits the page in its own words, "writing and artwork by Banksy" set against "additional photography by" four other people, the colophon itself drawing the line between the artwork and the photograph of it. By 2004 the separate photography credit is gone and the work is simply "by Banksy." Whatever one concludes about hands on stencils, the books' own front matter shows the image-author and the artist-name were not one entity at the start, and that the distinction was first separated out and then dissolved into the single name. This is a description of three colophons, not a charge.

Connective logic (DOCUMENTED as pattern, CODEX as claim): the principals entered through the lens because in-situ documentation is what survives a street piece left to degrade. The photograph is the durable object; the credit page is the title deed.

Adjacent context, not part of the spine: in 2004 Pictures on Walls issued the under-publicised Pictures of Walls, which is what its title says, and which marks the practice's standing interest in the wall as subject. Held alongside, not inside, the credit argument.

How this bears on the present sales

The 2026 life-size and the found-landscape were sold as singular authored paintings. The credit trail is the deep-background reason the question "authored by whom, exactly" is not idle: the artist's own early books show the image-authorship of this body of work was, at the outset, credited to others and only later consolidated under the single name now driving the prices. The market sells one author. The colophons started with several.
